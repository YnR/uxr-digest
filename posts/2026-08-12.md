## 1. AI in the Research Practice

**"What Star Wars Got Right About AI" — UX Collective (Aug 11)**
Patrick Neeman argues that the useful mental model for AI in 2026 isn't Star Trek's single omniscient computer — it's Star Wars' cheap, task-specific droids: bought, sold, and argued with like appliances, but constantly underfoot. For researchers, this reframes the question from "will AI replace us?" to "which droids are worth deploying for which tasks?" A useful mindset reset for practitioners overwhelmed by the volume of tooling. ([Read →](https://uxdesign.cc/what-star-wars-got-right-about-ai-6404ae7c2e62))

## 3. Job Market for UX Researchers

**"The Loom That Raised Its Hand" — UX Collective (Aug ~9)**
Takuma Kakehi synthesizes sobering employment data: Harvard researchers found that at firms adopting generative AI, junior headcount fell 7.7% relative to non-adopters within six quarters, while senior headcount kept climbing. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab reports a 16% relative employment decline for workers aged 22–25 in the most AI-exposed occupations since late 2022. The pattern — AI compressing the bottom of career ladders while senior roles grow — is a direct signal for early-career UX researchers.
