## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[Do AI Moderators Uncover as Much as Human Moderators?](https://measuringu.com/do-ai-moderators-uncover-as-much-as-human-moderators/) — MeasuringU (Nov 3, 2026)**
Jeff Sauro and colleagues publish the third entry in MeasuringU's AI moderation series, this time asking not how much participants say (covered in September) but whether what they say to an AI moderator is as useful as what they say to a human. The team built and ran their own AI moderator and collected direct comparative data — a rare empirical contribution in a space where most claims are based on vendor marketing or anecdote. For practitioners fielding internal pressure to cut moderation costs by switching to AI tools, this piece offers actual numbers rather than intuitions; the caveats about implementation specifics are worth reading carefully before generalizing the findings to other platforms.

**[How to Build an LLM Grader From a Rubric](https://saeidehbakhshi.substack.com/p/how-to-build-an-llm-grader-from-a) — Research toolbox (Nov 2, 2026)**
Part 8 of Saeideh Bakhshi's AI Evals series works through how to construct and test an LLM grader from an existing evaluation rubric — connecting the rubric-writing and grader-design threads from earlier parts of the series. The piece is practical and step-by-step: it shows how to translate a qualitative rubric into a form an LLM can apply consistently, then validate that it does. For researchers already building LLM-assisted analysis pipelines, or considering them, this is the most concrete how-to in the series to date. Note: this is a paid post for subscribers.
