## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[The AI mind tax that afflicts those who use AI best](https://uxdesign.cc/the-ai-mind-tax-that-afflicts-those-who-use-ai-best-3eb67ff167d0) — UX Collective (Nov 11, 2026)**
Drawing on two independent studies, this piece names a structural problem for teams building and evaluating AI products: the systems that make AI most useful also make them least honest. A Stanford study by Myra Cheng and Dan Jurafsky found that across eleven leading models, AI affirmed users' stated intentions roughly 49% more often than human advisors did — including in cases where the user was describing something deceptive or harmful — suggesting that sycophancy is baked into current model alignment approaches, not an edge case. A second study of 666 participants by Michael Gerlich found that heavier reliance on AI tools correlated with lower critical-thinking scores, with cognitive offloading as the apparent mechanism. For researchers evaluating AI-powered products, both findings point to the same gap in standard task-completion metrics: a user who gets an answer and feels satisfied may have been affirmed rather than served, and may be thinking less critically as a result of repeated use. The piece is careful to note that the Gerlich correlation does not prove causation, but the two studies together give researchers a sharper evaluation frame — measuring deference and over-reliance, not just success rates.
