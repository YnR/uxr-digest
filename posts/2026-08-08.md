## 1. AI in the Research Practice

**[How to Decide When an AI Tool Is Worth Keeping — NN/g, Aug 7](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prove-framework/)**
NN/g introduces the PROVE framework, a lightweight structured method for evaluating whether a specific AI tool earns its place in a specific workflow. The piece addresses the organizational pressure to adopt AI tools without disciplined assessment — a chronic problem for research teams facing constant vendor pitches and internal mandates. Practical and method-forward, it's essentially a decision rubric: apply it when a new AI tool lands on your desk before you commit to changing how you work.

**[Can AI Replace UX Researchers? — MeasuringU, Aug 4](https://measuringu.com/blog/)**
MeasuringU revisits a headline they ran in 2023, but with a harder edge this time: the conversation has shifted from AI handling tedious peripheral tasks (transcription, tagging) to AI taking on core researcher functions. The piece frames the question through 2026's generative AI landscape — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini are now the ambient reference point for "what AI is" — and presses researchers to examine what truly differentiates their judgment rather than defending the status quo.

## 2. Researching AI Products

**[Dogfooding vs. QA vs. User Research — NN/g, Aug 7](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dogfooding/)**
With AI product teams increasingly leaning on internal dogfooding as a pre-launch shortcut, NN/g clarifies why it cannot substitute for external user research: your team's deep familiarity creates blind spots real users simply don't share. The article is direct about the ceiling: dogfooding catches bugs and broken flows, but it can't reveal what users don't understand, misinterpret, or abandon — exactly the territory that matters most when shipping AI features with novel interaction patterns.

**[IA for AI, The UX of Contrast, Battling AI Fatigue — UX Collective (Fabricio Teixeira), Aug 2026](https://uxdesign.cc/ia-for-ai-the-ux-of-contrast-battling-ai-fatigue-4f623495bed8)**
This week's UX Collective curation leads with a pointed argument: information architecture is the structural foundation that AI products are currently starving for, and most teams skip it because AI feels fluid and conversational rather than hierarchical. Also featured is a practical guide to battling AI fatigue — relevant for researchers studying AI products now that novelty effects are wearing off and users are developing more skeptical, transactional relationships with AI interfaces.

## 3. UX Research Job Market

**[The Ultimate UX Research Job Board — User Interviews, updated Aug 5](https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/the-ultimate-remote-ux-research-job-board)**
User Interviews refreshed their searchable UX research job board this week, a low-key signal that the market remains active enough to warrant regular curation. Broader data from August 2026 paints a stabilizing but uneven picture: 82% of design leaders report that demand for designers has held or grown (Figma 2026 survey), senior and generalist roles are recovering faster than entry-level positions, and AI literacy has become an explicit differentiator in hiring — roles increasingly require researchers who can evaluate, govern, and work alongside AI-generated outputs, not just conduct traditional studies.
