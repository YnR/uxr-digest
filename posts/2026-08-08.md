## 1. AI in the Research Practice

**[How to Decide When an AI Tool Is Worth Keeping — NN/g, Aug 7](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prove-framework/)**
NN/g introduces the PROVE framework, a lightweight structured method for evaluating whether a specific AI tool earns its place in a specific workflow. The piece addresses the organizational pressure to adopt AI tools without disciplined assessment — a chronic problem for research teams facing constant vendor pitches and internal mandates. Practical and method-forward: apply it when a new AI tool lands on your desk before you commit to changing how you work.

---

## 2. Researching AI Products

**[Dogfooding vs. QA vs. User Research — NN/g, Aug 7](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dogfooding/)**
With AI product teams increasingly leaning on internal dogfooding as a pre-launch shortcut, NN/g clarifies why it cannot substitute for external user research: your team's deep familiarity creates blind spots real users simply don't share. Dogfooding catches bugs and broken flows, but it can't reveal what users don't understand, misinterpret, or abandon — exactly the territory that matters most when shipping AI features with novel interaction patterns.

## 3. UX Research Job Market

**[The Ultimate UX Research Job Board — User Interviews, updated Aug 5](https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/the-ultimate-remote-ux-research-job-board)**
User Interviews refreshed their searchable UX research job board. The update reflects a stabilising but uneven picture: senior and generalist roles are recovering faster than entry-level positions, and AI literacy is now an explicit differentiator in hiring rather than a bonus.
