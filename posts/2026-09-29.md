## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[How We Save Entry-Level UX Work That Teaches Craft in the Time of AI](https://uxdesign.cc/how-we-save-entry-level-ux-work-that-teaches-craft-in-the-time-of-ai-our-field-depends-on-this-e1684a0d67eb) — Patrick Neeman, UX Collective (~Sep 27–29, 2026)**
This is the practical follow-on to Neeman's earlier piece on senior practitioners and junior cultivation (covered Sep 23). Where that piece made the structural argument, this one names the seven tasks that AI has automated away — transcript tagging, first-pass wireframes, screener drafts, competitive audits, redline sheets, meeting notes — and proposes concrete guardrails to put them back: assigning every junior a domain with a real business owner who will take the meetings, and pricing "unbundled training time" at ~10% senior overhead on any project where a junior holds the decision role. For UX researchers, the direct parallel is synthesis work: if AI handles first-pass clustering and teams never push back, the pipeline of researchers who know how to interpret raw data shrinks — and that's a strategic risk worth raising explicitly with research leads.
