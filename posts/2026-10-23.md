## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[User Experience Salaries & Calculator (2026)](https://measuringu.com/user-experience-salaries-calculator-2026/) — MeasuringU (Oct 22, 2026)**
Jeff Sauro, Jim Lewis, and Will Schiavone released MeasuringU's 2026 salary analysis, combining the UXPA's 2024 salary survey with a parallel 2024 User Interviews survey to reach 1,063 responses — more than double any previous edition. The combined dataset reports a median of $124K for male respondents and $111K for female respondents, a gap within the margin of error but consistent with prior years. The calculator lets practitioners estimate expected compensation by role, experience, education, and region. The accompanying analysis notes that while nominal salaries have risen since the 2022 survey, real purchasing power has largely kept pace with inflation rather than outpacing it.

**[You Can Become a Better Researcher and Still Become Less Necessary](https://saeidehbakhshi.substack.com/p/you-can-become-a-better-researcher) — Research toolbox (Oct 23, 2026)**
Saeideh Bakhshi (lead user researcher for model behavior at OpenAI) argues that AI making research easier to execute does not automatically translate into researchers gaining more strategic influence — for practitioners whose work consists mainly of running studies others requested, AI may simply make that contribution easier for teams to route around. The post identifies high-leverage work that is harder to substitute: surfacing problems no one asked about, changing how a team measures success, and staying involved after a decision to track its real-world consequences. The implicit job-market signal is that demand will bifurcate: researchers who generate strategic insight will remain valuable, while execution-only work faces commoditization pressure.
