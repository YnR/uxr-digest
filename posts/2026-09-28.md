## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[How We Save Entry-Level UX Work That Teaches Craft in the Time of AI](https://uxdesign.cc/how-we-save-entry-level-ux-work-that-teaches-craft-in-the-time-of-ai-our-field-depends-on-this-e1684a0d67eb) — Patrick Neeman, UX Collective (Sep 28, 2026)**
Neeman documents the structural problem AI has created for junior UX education: tasks like transcript tagging and competitive audits have been handed to AI not because they're unimportant, but because AI is fast at them — and nobody recorded what the *doing* of those tasks actually taught. The specific loss he names is diagnostic intuition: a researcher who tags transcripts learns to recognise when a participant says "it's fine" but means "I gave up," a signal no AI currently flags. Two data points anchor the urgency — Maze's Future of User Research Report 2026 finds that 69% of research professionals now use AI in at least some projects (up 19 points year-on-year), and Brynjolfsson et al's payroll data through June 2026 shows employment for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations running 19% below where it would be without AI exposure. Neeman proposes seven tactics for preserving craft-level learning — including deliberate "slow reviews" of AI-generated outputs and pairing junior researchers with the AI as the one who checks and challenges the synthesis rather than the one who produces it.
