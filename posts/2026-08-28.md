## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**Designing for the Proxy: Why AI Is Changing Who Reads First** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/designing-for-the-proxy-2606ffac2335) *(Aug 2026)*

Aurélie Radom traces how each new medium has introduced a fresh intermediary between creators and audiences: search engines rewarded discoverability, social platforms rewarded engagement, and now large language models reward interpretability. When a user asks an AI assistant a question, the content that reaches them has already been filtered, summarised, and repackaged by the model — meaning the LLM is effectively the "first reader," and how well content is structured for machine interpretation now determines whether it reaches humans at all. For UX researchers studying AI-powered products, the piece sharpens a practical evaluation problem: when AI agents mediate the experience, task-completion studies and screen-level heuristics capture only the visible surface, while the real challenge is the delegation chain — whether the model correctly interprets, retrieves, and represents information in ways that preserve user intent and trust. The proxy framing gives researchers a useful concept for scoping evaluation criteria on RAG-backed or AI-orchestrated features, where the "user" the system is optimising for may not be the human at the end of the chain.
