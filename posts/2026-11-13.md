## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[The Research Tooling Industry Is Selling Us Methodological Shortcuts](https://saeidehbakhshi.substack.com/p/ux-research-tools-methodological-risks) — Research toolbox (Nov 12, 2026)**
Saeideh Bakhshi's latest argues that research software vendors have become very good at selling efficiency while quietly removing the methodological decisions that give research its validity. The concern is structural: when a tool applies defaults that may not fit the research question, hides its assumptions, or is aimed at users without formal research training, those shortcuts don't just affect one study — they scale across every team that uses the platform. Bakhshi's recommendation is that research leaders treat tool selection as a methodological decision in its own right, applying the same scrutiny to what a platform assumes and omits as they would to a study design. Teams that delegate research tasks to AI tools face a parallel accountability question: who owns the methodological choices when the software has already made them?

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[AI is the new old design material](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-is-the-new-old-design-material-0e7f83540c9a) — UX Collective (Nov 12, 2026)**
Drawing on Figma's 2026 AI report — which found the share of designers taking part in development roughly doubled in a year, from 21% to 41% — this piece argues that AI is best understood as a material designers must learn by building, not an automation layer applied to existing workflows. The implications for research are concrete: as AI collapses the boundary between design and development, the team configurations, roles, and hand-off points that standard research questions assume (who made this decision? who can change it?) are becoming less stable. Researchers evaluating AI-powered products increasingly need study designs that can account for a product whose shape is partly determined by the material properties of the model underneath it, not just the choices its designers made.
