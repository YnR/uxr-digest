## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[Bottle Your Judgment and Make It Outlive You](https://uxdesign.cc/bottle-your-judgment-and-make-it-outlive-you-14f422772262) — UX Collective, Dan Maccarone (Aug 2026)**

Maccarone argues that AI has opened a genuinely new capability: packaging an individual practitioner's judgment — their decision-making process and the methodological heuristics they've spent a career building — into a system that others can access and apply. The point is not to replace anyone's thinking, but to make it portable and persistent. For research operations, this reframes a strategic question: teams shouldn't only ask which AI tools to adopt, but how to encode their own research standards and analytical frameworks into AI-assisted infrastructure before that expertise walks out the door.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[The Feature Was Fine. So Why Did Users Keep Hiding It?](https://uxdesign.cc/the-feature-was-fine-so-why-did-users-keep-hiding-it-58082b6f3abf) — UX Collective, SoYoon Park (Aug 17, 2026)**

Drawing on a KPMG and University of Melbourne study of 48,000 respondents across 47 countries, Park reports that 57% of employees admit to hiding their AI use at work — not because the tool fails functionally, but because using it conflicts with how they want to be seen. The article's central argument: "identity score," the degree to which a feature aligns with a user's self-image, predicts hiding behaviour better than functional quality does. For researchers evaluating AI-powered products, this adds a dimension that task-success rates and satisfaction surveys won't reach — adoption can fail silently through identity mismatch, invisible in standard metrics.
