## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**Streamlined Measurement of the UX of AI** — [MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/streamlined-measurement-of-the-ux-of-ai/) *(Aug 18)*

Jeff Sauro and colleagues publish a shortened, validated instrument for measuring the UX of AI systems across six constructs: AI Productivity, AI Trust, AI Dependency, AI Anxiety, AI Personification, and Early Adoption. The streamlined version keeps just two to three items per construct while preserving strong psychometric properties — coefficient alpha ranges from 0.81 to 0.87, comfortably above the 0.70 reliability threshold. This matters practically: researchers evaluating AI features often need to append UX measurement to studies already tight on participant time, and a 12–18 item battery that discriminates cleanly between constructs (e.g., separating trust from dependency, or productivity from personification) is far more usable than the original longer instruments. Worth downloading for anyone running quantitative studies on AI products.

**Is the future of software certain once AI is everywhere?** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/is-the-future-of-software-certain-once-ai-is-everywhere-f93e1bc5697f) *(Aug 19)*

Arin Bhowmick pushes back on the "SaaSpocalypse" narrative — that AI will replace application software wholesale — and reframes the shift as evolution rather than extinction: AI becomes the primary interface layer users talk to, while familiar applications recede into task-specific agents operating behind it. For UX researchers, the practical implication is a question about *what* to study when the visible interface is generative and the underlying app surface is agentic. Task-completion and screen-level heuristics translate poorly to that world; the unit of analysis shifts to whether the top-layer AI correctly delegates, interprets returns, and preserves user intent across a chain of hidden actions. This is a useful lens for teams starting to plan research on AI-orchestration products.
