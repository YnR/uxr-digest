## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**Artificial Intelligence: Glossary** — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/artificial-intelligence-glossary/) *(Aug 21)*

NN/g published a plain-language reference glossary covering the AI terms that come up most often in product and design work — tokens, context windows, agents, evals, prompt injection, and more. For researchers, this is useful less as a tutorial and more as shared vocabulary: when a stakeholder says "we should run evals on this," or an engineer talks about "grounding," having a common definition matters for scoping research questions accurately. The glossary is also a signal of where NN/g expects the practitioner baseline to land; the terms chosen indicate which concepts they consider essential fluency for UX work in 2026.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**AI-Generated Images Can Perform as Well as Stock Photography** — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-generated-images/) *(Aug 21)*

NN/g tested AI-generated vs. stock imagery on a fictional consulting firm's webpage with 77 participants, measuring perceived credibility and quality without disclosing image provenance. The headline finding: when users don't know an image is AI-generated, it creates no perceptual penalty compared to stock photography — the source of the image matters less than whether it fits its context. For researchers evaluating AI-content features, this is a useful calibration point: the "AI penalty" in user perception is largely a disclosure effect, not a quality effect, which has direct implications for how studies on AI-generated content are designed (provenance information in stimuli needs to be controlled explicitly).
