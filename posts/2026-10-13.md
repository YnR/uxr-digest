## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[AI is hollowing out the middle of design, leaving an hourglass behind](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-is-hollowing-out-the-middle-of-design-leaving-an-hourglass-behind-b7069db250fd) — UX Collective (Oct 12, 2026)**
A new UX Collective analysis argues that AI is concentrating design work at the two ends of the process — early discovery and final delivery — while hollowing out the middle stages where workshops, synthesis sessions, and research deliverables traditionally live. Drawing on a study of 31 designers, the author found AI involvement follows an hourglass shape: high at the start and end, dramatically lower in the middle — the Double Diamond's deliverable-heavy phases (journey maps, personas, presentations) have been accumulating overhead for 15 years while time for the actual product shrank. For UX researchers, the implication is direct: if AI automates the synthesis and documentation layer, the value proposition shifts toward upstream question-framing and downstream product-quality accountability. Teams building AI-assisted research workflows should audit where in their process they are most exposed to this compression.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[Secret agent bot: accessibility matters for the agents even if you can't see them](https://uxdesign.cc/secret-agent-bot-accessibility-matters-for-the-agents-even-if-you-cant-see-them-2f53a5670ec9) — UX Collective (Oct 13, 2026)**
Published today, this piece makes the case that the semantic structure screen readers rely on is the same layer AI agents use to perceive and interact with web pages — meaning accessibility and AI readability are now the same problem. The article explains that agents can read pages three ways (screenshots, raw HTML, or the accessibility tree), but the accessibility tree is the most common and reliable path; missing alt text, poor heading structure, and unlabeled form fields block AI agents as surely as they block screen readers. For researchers evaluating AI-powered products, this doubles the stakes of accessibility audits: a Cloudflare report from June 2026 noted that automated traffic had for the first time exceeded human traffic on the web, so the agent is now a primary "user" of many surfaces. Testing whether an AI assistant or agent can complete a task on a product is, in practice, also an accessibility evaluation.
