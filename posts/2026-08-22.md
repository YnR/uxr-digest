## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**Artificial Intelligence: Glossary** — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/artificial-intelligence-glossary/) *(Aug 21)*

NN/g publishes a plain-language reference covering the AI terminology that most frequently comes up in product and design work — tokens, context windows, agents, evals, prompt injection, hallucinations, fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation, and more. The definitions are written for practitioners, not engineers: each term is explained in terms of what it means for the design or research process rather than how the underlying mechanism works. For UX researchers, the practical value is twofold: it closes vocabulary gaps when working alongside AI engineering teams, and it anchors interview guides and research questions in language that doesn't inadvertently import engineering assumptions into user-facing prompts.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**AI-Generated Images Can Perform as Well as Stock Photography** — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-generated-images/) *(Aug 21)*

NN/g reports an experimental study comparing user responses to AI-generated images versus stock photography on company websites, presented without provenance labelling. When participants did not know whether an image was AI-generated, it produced no trust or authenticity penalty relative to stock — authenticity ratings were, in fact, marginally higher for AI images, though the difference was small (0.4 points on a 7-point scale). For researchers evaluating AI-powered products that generate or incorporate imagery, this establishes a useful baseline: unlabelled AI imagery does not, by itself, degrade perceived credibility. The article also flags the ethical dimension — provenance, consent, and policy considerations remain live issues separate from the perceptual findings — which points toward a research gap between what users detect and what they would *want* disclosed.
