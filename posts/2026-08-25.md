## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**Sterling Cooper Built Your UX Process. We Need to Redefine It in the Age of AI and Systems.** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/sterling-cooper-built-your-ux-process-we-need-to-redefine-it-in-the-age-of-ai-and-systems-520a2d3b2a60) *(Aug 23)*

Patrick Neeman argues that modern UX practice — including research practice — is still running on rituals inherited from 1960s ad agency culture: the big reveal, the persona deck, the research repository that becomes a graveyard of findings nobody revisits. The article makes the case that these inherited patterns are particularly mismatched for AI-era work, where the relevant question is no longer "did the presentation land?" but "what did the work actually move?" For UX researchers, the most pointed observation is about research repositories: artefacts that accumulate findings but fail to connect them to decisions are a structural problem that AI tools alone can't fix — they require rethinking the purpose of research output from archive to active influence.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**We Used to Log Off** — [UX Collective Newsletter](https://newsletter.uxdesign.cc/p/we-used-to-log-off) *(Aug 24)*

Fabricio Teixeira's weekly dispatch opens with a telling observation: opening a laptop after a few days away and finding multiple AI agents paused mid-task — taxes, landscaping ideas, policy documents — each waiting to continue a thread that started without a fixed end. He frames this as the 2026 equivalent of too many browser tabs, except the tabs have been doing things in your absence. For researchers studying AI-powered products, this vignette crystallises a real methodological challenge: when AI runs persistently in the background rather than on demand, the unit of experience is no longer a discrete session or task completion — it's an ongoing, fragmented relationship with a system that acts between interactions. Standard task-based usability protocols don't capture that, and most diary or experience-sampling designs aren't set up to catch it either.
