## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[Nothing has changed other than how we talk about it](https://uxdesign.cc/nothing-has-changed-other-than-how-we-talk-about-it-fcc554e853aa) — Tom Green, UX Collective (Sep 18, 2026)**
Tom Green argues that agentic AI workflows don't actually change what makes UX work good — judgment, correction, and human oversight remain non-negotiable. The piece walks through a practical example of handing a brief to an AI agent to generate a structured PRD, then shows where human correction is required before anything advances. A useful grounding read for practitioners who feel pressure to hand more of the process to agents than they should.

**[How to use scheduled tasks in Claude](https://www.userresearchstrategist.com/p/how-to-use-scheduled-tasks-in-claude) — Nikki Anderson, User Research Strategist (Sep 17, 2026)**
Nikki Anderson provides a hands-on walkthrough of using Claude's scheduled tasks feature for recurring research workflows, illustrating three UXR-specific skill configurations and their components. The practical framing — automating digest prep, stakeholder updates, and synthesis nudges — makes this directly actionable for solo researchers and small teams. An accompanying live workshop on "Claude Skills for User Research" is scheduled for September 22.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[Altman and Amodei's "unsafe at any prompt" moment for AI](https://uxdesign.cc/altman-and-amodeis-unsafe-at-any-prompt-moment-for-ai-6a73f144b56e) — Patrick Neeman, UX Collective (Sep 18, 2026)**
Patrick Neeman draws a pointed analogy between today's AI industry and Detroit's decades-long habit of blaming drivers rather than fixing unsafe cars — arguing that AI product teams, including researchers, need to treat safety and failure modes as design problems, not user-education problems. The piece is a useful provocation for any team scoping research for AI features: if your research plan doesn't include explicit studies of failure, harm, and blame attribution, this suggests it should. Context is the recent public statements from Altman and Amodei on capability pacing and third-party safety evaluators.

## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[A Proposal for Unpacking Human-Centered Roles](https://www.dscout.com/people-nerds/human-centered-roles) — dscout People Nerds (Sep 16, 2026)**
Dscout makes the case that the proliferation of research-adjacent titles — UX researcher, insights analyst, design researcher, CX strategist, and so on — is causing real friction in hiring, career progression, and internal advocacy. The piece proposes a tighter taxonomy to help practitioners clarify their positioning and help organizations understand what they're actually hiring for. Relevant for anyone navigating the current job market or building a research team.
