## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[Agent Harness: Definition](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/agent-harness/) — Nielsen Norman Group (Nov 6, 2026)**
Caleb Sponheim defines "agent harness" as the software layer that wraps an AI model and runs its execution loop: it manages the context the model sees, calls the tools the model selects, enforces permissions before each call, and returns results back to the model. The harness is what determines what an AI product can actually do and how much of the work users are still expected to handle themselves — it sets the floor and ceiling on agent autonomy more concretely than the model's raw capabilities do. For research teams evaluating or designing AI-powered products, this framing shifts the analytical target: behavior observed in usability sessions is a product of harness design as much as model design, and the two have different error modes and intervention points. The article also notes that while the harness can enforce hard limits, the model may not reliably follow behavioral instructions — a distinction with direct implications for predicting and testing AI product behavior.

## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[After the AI Bubble: A Manifesto](https://uxdesign.cc/after-the-ai-bubble-a-manifesto-2e3157387662) — UX Collective (Nov 7, 2026)**
A group of designers and technologists published a manifesto on UX Collective arguing that the current AI boom shares the structural features of earlier speculative tech bubbles, and that the fatalism it generates — the widespread message that workers must adopt AI or be left behind — is manufactured rather than inevitable. The piece names specific harms worth resisting: AI personas engineered to simulate consciousness, AI tools forced on employees without genuine consent, and information environments that deliberately deepen confusion. For UX researchers thinking about their professional positioning, the manifesto reframes a question job-posting data alone cannot answer: not just whether AI is changing who gets hired, but whether the normative pressure driving those changes is as fixed and irreversible as it is being presented. Its practical ask — that designers continue to build software that fits how people actually think — also signals what research methods and research questions will carry weight once the current speculative cycle runs its course.
