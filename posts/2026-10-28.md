## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[AI Engagement Quantitative UX: 2 High-Level Takeaways from 20+ Charts](https://baymard.com/blog) — Baymard Institute (Oct 27, 2026)**
Baymard published the first quantitative survey of online shoppers' AI engagement, covering 20+ data points on AI feature usage, trust and privacy concerns, perceived accuracy of AI-generated content, confidence in AI-generated images, how much purchasing authority shoppers are willing to grant AI, preferences for AI versus human support, and who shoppers hold responsible when an AI purchasing error occurs. The data is survey-based and designed to complement Baymard's qualitative usability testing rather than replace it. For teams designing or evaluating AI features in ecommerce, this is an unusually broad benchmark dataset covering both functional and attitudinal dimensions — particularly useful for framing stakeholder conversations about where users currently place their trust.

## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[User Experience Salaries & Calculator (2026)](https://measuringu.com/salary-survey2026/) — MeasuringU (Oct 26–27, 2026)**
Jeff Sauro, Jim Lewis, and Will Schiavone published MeasuringU's 2026 UX salary report, conducted in partnership with UXPA and drawing on 383 responses from 24 countries (82% U.S., up from 67% in 2024). The headline median of $143K is a nominal 19% increase from the 2024 figure of $120K, but the authors note the sample shift toward U.S. respondents inflated the number; across five profiled example roles, inflation-adjusted pay actually fell by roughly $6,300 on average. Location remains the single biggest driver (U.S. median $153K; UK $100K; Canada $81K; Germany $68K), followed by job level, with senior practitioners substantially outpacing entry-level peers. For practitioners benchmarking their pay or evaluating a move, the salary calculator on the same page is worth using directly — it lets you control for role, experience, location, and education to produce a personalized estimate.
