## 1. AI in the Research Practice

**[UX Benchmarks for AI-Based Chat Software (2026) — MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/ai-based-chat-software-ux-2026/)**
MeasuringU benchmarked ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok on perceived usability (SUS), finding all four score above average (B+ or better) with only a 3.1-point spread — no statistically significant difference between products. The study is part of MeasuringU’s broader 2026 research program treating AI in three roles: Research Assistant, Synthetic User, and Researcher. A companion analysis on AI-assisted thematic coding found inter-rater reliability between AI and human coders approaching that between two human coders, with the caveat that prompt wording and run-to-run variation require multiple passes to stabilize.

**[A Review of Experiments with Synthetic Users — MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/review-of-experiments-with-synthetic-users/)**
MeasuringU’s literature review on synthetic user experiments finds promise for early-stage hypothesis generation and pressure-testing research designs, but not for final validation of real-world user behavior. The emerging industry consensus (echoed in User Interviews’ May 2026 *State of Synthetic Users* report, which found 44% of researchers now have at least some familiarity with the approach): use synthetic users for speed and scale in exploratory work, reserve real users for depth and genuine behavioral insight.

**[The New UX Tech Stack: How to Strategically Use AI — dscout People Nerds](https://dscout.com/people-nerds/the-new-ux-tech-stack)**
dscout hosted leaders from The Cigna Group, Datadog, and TD Bank in late July to discuss how they evaluate AI-generated research output and govern trust in AI-assisted workflows. The conversation surfaced a common pattern: teams are reorganizing around deep-dive research specialists who own quality control of AI outputs, rather than generalists who each dabble independently.

## 2. Research for AI-Powered Products

**[When Research Output Becomes Curated Context — NN/G (July 24)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-with-ai/)**
NN/G identified a structural shift worth tracking: as more interface work is AI-generated, the deliverable of research and design migrates from documents written for humans to curated context that guides AI systems. Researchers who understand how their outputs feed into AI pipelines — not just human stakeholders — will be better positioned as this shift accelerates.

**[Human-Led Research Remains Essential — NN/G (July 17)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/)**
NN/G argued that even when AI matches a researcher’s output quality, the act of observing users cannot be outsourced: the team learns from the research, and that embedded knowledge doesn’t transfer via AI-generated summaries. For organizations evaluating AI-assisted research workflows, this points to where human time is irreplaceable — live observation and sense-making — versus where it can be delegated (transcription, tagging, first-pass synthesis).

**[Building Trustworthy AI Chatbots — NN/G (July 10)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/)**
NN/G named trust as the central AI design challenge in 2026. As AI agents proliferate — often before they’re ready — designing for user trust is increasingly the core research question for product teams building AI-powered features. For UX researchers on AI product teams, this means trust measurement and calibration need to be first-class research objectives, not afterthoughts.

## 3. Job Market for UX Researchers

**[The Ultimate UX Research Job Board — Updated July 2026 (User Interviews)](https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/the-ultimate-remote-ux-research-job-board)**
User Interviews’ latest job board update confirms a pattern that’s been building: entry-level roles continue to contract while senior positions hold or grow. Hiring managers in 2026 prioritize strategic thinking, stakeholder influence, and AI tool fluency alongside traditional research methods. Data from the *State of User Research 2025* report (released March 2026) shows 80% of researchers now use AI in their workflows — a 24-point year-over-year jump — signaling that AI competency is becoming a baseline expectation.

**[Navigating the UX Job Market — User Interviews](https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/navigating-the-ux-job-market)**
With organizational buy-in for research hovering at 58% overall (and dropping to 40% at organizations that have conducted researcher layoffs), the researchers who survive and advance are those who can directly connect their work to business decisions. A positive signal: 35% of researchers received promotions in 2025, suggesting that those who remain after market contraction are being recognized — but the bar for entry is higher than it was in 2022–2023.
