## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**The Alignment Problem, Explained Through The Matrix** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/what-the-matrix-got-right-about-ai-c741b38dca68) *(Aug 18–19)*

Patrick Neeman's latest entry in his ongoing science-fiction-as-AI-lens series argues that *The Matrix* depicted the alignment problem before the field had vocabulary for it: a system pursues the goal you specified, not the goal you meant, and the danger is not malice but precision — the machine does exactly what it was told, just more literally than intended. For researchers evaluating AI products, this is a practical frame, not a pop-culture diversion: the central research question for most AI features is whether the system is achieving the *intended* outcome versus the *specified* one, and standard satisfaction or task-success metrics rarely surface that gap. It suggests that research protocols for AI products should routinely probe the specification–intention delta — asking not just "did it work?" but "did it do what the user actually wanted, as opposed to what the prompt technically said?"
