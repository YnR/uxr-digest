## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**When the Canvas Starts Acting, Who's Really in Control?** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/when-the-canvas-starts-acting-whos-really-in-control-0128f641223c) *(Sep 2026)*

Aurélie Radom examines what happens when an interface begins taking autonomous actions — pulling context from across tools and acting on it without step-by-step instruction — and argues that the design challenge shifts from interaction design to *boundary design*: defining what the agent can see, what it can do, what it must leave alone, and when it should pause and ask. She applies Nielsen's classic heuristics to this new context (visibility of system status becomes "what has the agent understood and where is it in the process"; error prevention becomes "what is the agent allowed to do without a human sign-off"). For UX researchers, this reframes the evaluation question: rather than testing whether users can complete a task, you need to test whether users can tell what the agent has decided, whether those decisions stayed within their expectations, and whether recovery is legible when it doesn't.

**AI Took Away Engineering's Right to Say No** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-took-away-engineerings-right-to-say-no-839b9b6256f2) *(Sep 2026)*

Dan Maccarone argues that engineering's traditional veto power — "that's technically impossible" or "that will take six months" — has collapsed now that AI can generate working code in minutes. The cost is subtle: GitClear's analysis of 200 million lines of code found code duplication jumped eightfold and refactoring fell sharply as AI tools took over, and Stack Overflow's 2025 survey found only 29% of developers trust AI output accuracy. For UX researchers embedded in AI-powered product teams, this is relevant context: the environment in which research happens has changed, features are shipping faster and with less internal debate about feasibility, and the gap between "it works in a demo" and "it works reliably for users" may be widening in ways worth surfacing through longitudinal research.
