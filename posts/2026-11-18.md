## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[Everyone knows sparkles mean AI. Use them to make design audits easier](https://uxdesign.cc/everyone-knows-sparkles-mean-ai-use-them-to-make-design-audits-easier-bdacc76cef11) — UX Collective (Nov 17, 2026)**
The sparkle icon has become a universally recognized signal for AI-powered features, and this piece argues that researchers and designers can exploit that shared convention to structure design audits more efficiently. By using the sparkle as a tagging mechanism during heuristic review, teams can quickly inventory which UI elements rely on AI, making it easier to scope an audit of an AI-powered product and track coverage. The practical implication for researchers is that a visual convention designed for end users can double as an audit organizing principle — reducing cognitive overhead when cataloguing a complex, AI-saturated interface.

## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[What is a researcher's worth?](https://uxdesign.cc/what-is-a-researchers-worth-ea14c95c6d12) — UX Collective (Nov 17, 2026)**
This piece examines why the debate over what a UX researcher is actually worth resists easy answers. As AI tools absorb more of the operational layer of research — transcription, synthesis, screener writing — the question of researcher value is shifting from "how much can they produce?" to "what judgment can they supply that the tool cannot?" The article frames the difficulty as partly epistemological: research value is generated in the interaction between insight and decision-making context, which is hard to quantify and often invisible to the stakeholders doing the budgeting. For researchers tracking the job market, this is a useful frame for the ongoing squeeze on headcount: the debate is not settled, but its terms are changing.
