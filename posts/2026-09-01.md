## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**UX in 2027 May Be Less About Interfaces and More About Behavior** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/ux-trends-2027-beyond-interface-design-23e55b1daf07) *(Aug 31, 2026)*

As software increasingly interprets user goals, assembles its own interfaces, and acts on a person's behalf, this article argues that UX is shifting from defining predetermined paths to defining the *boundaries* within which a system generates its own paths. New interaction primitives — permission, reversibility, discoverability of agent capability — are emerging to replace traditional navigation and affordance patterns. For UX researchers, this reframes what to evaluate: task-completion and usability metrics assume a fixed interface, but adaptive systems require assessment of whether the agent stayed within expected behavioral boundaries and whether users could understand and recover from what the system decided. Gartner's projection (cited in the piece) that 40% of enterprise applications will include AI agents by end of 2026 adds urgency to developing evaluation frameworks for this interaction mode.

**The Call for an Agentic Standard: We Need to Stop Shipping the Same Form Four Different Times** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/the-call-for-an-agentic-standard-we-need-to-stop-shipping-the-same-form-four-different-times-be5b9d40e370) *(Sep 1, 2026)*

Patrick Neeman draws a direct analogy between today's fragmented agentic UI landscape — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, and Slack each rendering agent-generated interfaces differently — and the browser wars of the early web. The argument is that without a shared standard for how agents surface UI (whether via IFRAMEs, JSON-driven local renderers like A2UI, or other mechanisms), product teams will keep rebuilding the same interaction for each platform. For UX researchers, this fragmentation has an immediate methodological implication: usability findings and mental model research conducted on one agentic surface may not transfer to another, meaning multi-platform agent studies require explicit platform-as-variable treatment rather than treating "the agent interface" as a single, stable thing to evaluate.
