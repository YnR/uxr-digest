## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[How to Write an AI Evaluation Rubric: Criteria and Scoring](https://saeidehbakhshi.substack.com/p/how-to-write-a-good-ai-eval-rubric) — Research toolbox (c. Nov 4, 2026)**
Saeideh Bakhshi lays out an eight-step methodology for turning research judgment into a reusable rubric that an LLM can apply consistently: define successful performance, specify observable evidence, set meaningful scoring levels, build in a `cannot_judge` option for cases with insufficient evidence, and run a pilot before treating scores as valid data. A recurring caution runs through the piece — reviewers (human and model) tend to reward surface polish over factual accuracy, and the same underlying error can get penalized multiple times if criteria overlap — so the rubric design work is as much about preventing those biases as it is about codifying standards. This is part of Bakhshi's ongoing AI Evals series, which is building toward a practitioner's end-to-end workflow for using LLMs as qualitative analysis tools in research pipelines. The piece was published around November 4 and was not surfaced by earlier digest runs.

## Focus Area 3: UX Research Job Market

**[Five pairs of hands: what the typesetters can teach designers about AI](https://uxdesign.cc/five-pairs-of-hands-what-the-typesetters-can-teach-designers-about-ai-9d6b8e8c2285) — UX Collective (Nov 9, 2026)**
In a conversation with Christian Crumlish, UX Collective draws a direct line from 1990s desktop publishing — where one person with QuarkXPress absorbed the work of word processor, typesetter, and layout artist — to what AI is doing now across product teams. Crumlish's argument is structural: companies may look at the designers, researchers, product managers, and engineers on a team and decide they need fewer people, each covering more ground, not because any individual's skills became less valuable but because the economic case for the previous division of labor collapsed. The implication for researchers is not primarily about individual tool adoption but about headcount math at the team level — the same dynamic that eliminated typesetter roles at publishers is now operating on the product development org chart. Crumlish's own shop, Piper Morgan, runs on eleven AI agents with himself as the sole human, which he offers as a proof-of-concept rather than a prescription.
