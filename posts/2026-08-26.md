## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**When Everyone Can Generate Insights, What Matters Most?** — [People Nerds (dscout)](https://dscout.com/people-nerds/when-everyone-can-generate-insights-what-matters-most) *(Aug 19)*

As AI tools push insight generation into every corner of a product org — researchers, designers, PMs, and engineers can all now summarize interviews and surface patterns in minutes — a sharper version of the trust problem emerges: when insights are cheap to produce, it becomes harder to know which ones are worth acting on. The piece (featuring contributors from OpenAI, Meta, and Dropbox, among others) argues that the differentiating skill for UX researchers is no longer synthesis speed but trustworthiness: the ability to source-check, flag confounders, and stand behind a finding under scrutiny. For practitioners thinking about their role in an AI-saturated org, this reframe — from insight generator to insight quality guarantor — is both a threat and an opportunity.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**AI-Generated Images Can Perform as Well as Stock Photography** — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-generated-images/) *(Aug 21)*

NN/g tested whether users penalise AI-generated website imagery relative to stock photography when they don't know the image source, and found no meaningful perception gap: participants rated companies' trustworthiness, professionalism, and authenticity similarly regardless of whether the images were AI-generated or stock. The practical implication for product teams is that user perception concerns may not be a sound reason to avoid AI imagery — at least in contexts where provenance is not disclosed. For researchers evaluating AI-generated content in products more broadly, the study is a useful methodological data point: it suggests perception effects often depend heavily on disclosure framing rather than the inherent quality of AI output, which has implications for how to design and analyse studies of any AI-generated content.
