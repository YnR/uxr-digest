## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**Designers Are Becoming Toolmakers** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/designs-dreaded-phrase-is-coming-back-this-time-we-re-in-control-ab0c6996dc1e) *(Aug 12)*

Drawing on the 2026 AI in Design report, Kai Wong argues that practitioners are shifting from tool users to toolmakers — building bespoke tools and encoding their judgment, taste, and process into custom AI-augmented workflows. This mirrors an emerging arc in research operations: the next competitive advantage is not which off-the-shelf AI tool a team uses, but whether they've embedded their methodological standards into the infrastructure itself.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**Trust Transfer and the Risk of Borrowed Credibility** — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/were-gorging-on-borrowed-trust-and-it-s-going-to-cost-us-2bc3f918caf5) *(Aug 13)*

Dan Maccarone writes about how users apply brand trust to AI systems without scrutinising the machine itself — the more trusted the name, the less they question the output. Standard satisfaction metrics will not surface the gap between felt trust and warranted trust. Researchers studying AI products need methods that specifically probe trust calibration, not just task success or satisfaction scores.
