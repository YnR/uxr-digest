## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[ChatGPT vs Humans for Coding Open-Ended Responses, Three Years Later](https://measuringu.com/chatgpt-vs-humans-for-coding-open-ended-responses-three-years-later/) — Will Schiavone, Eva Sundberg & Jeff Sauro, MeasuringU (Sep 29, 2026)**
MeasuringU revisited their 2023 benchmark comparing ChatGPT with human coders on open-ended survey responses, this time using ChatGPT-5.6. The model now matches human interrater agreement (kappa .749 vs. .632 with GPT-4), meeting the threshold for "substantial" agreement, and goes further than humans by spontaneously subdividing core themes into fine-grained subcategories. The practical upshot for practitioners: AI-assisted qualitative coding has cleared the reliability bar, making it defensible to use as a first-pass or parallel-coding tool on large datasets. The remaining question the authors flag is whether finer granularity from the model helps or adds noise — worth piloting on your own data before adopting wholesale.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[Headless AI Will Make Every Product a Platform. Here's What to Consider When Designing for This.](https://uxdesign.cc/headless-ai-will-make-every-product-a-platform-heres-what-to-consider-when-designing-for-this-f219c33245c7) — UX Collective (Sep 29, 2026)**
This piece argues that as AI agents increasingly act as intermediaries — assembling flows, invoking APIs, and bypassing traditional UIs — the "user experience" researchers study becomes harder to observe directly. The article notes that 52% of crawler requests were for AI training by June 2026 (up from 22% in spring 2025), signaling that AI-mediated product use is no longer hypothetical. For researchers, the implication is structural: task-based usability studies assume a human operating a visible interface, a model that breaks when agents are the proximate actors. The piece doesn't propose new research methods, but the framing of products-as-APIs is a useful provocation for scoping what to evaluate in agentic product contexts.
