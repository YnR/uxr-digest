## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[The Role of UX in Agentic Software Factories](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-agentic-software-factories/) — Nielsen Norman Group (Oct 30, 2026)**
Tanner Kohler responds directly to Anthropic's published vision of an AI-native software development lifecycle — and to similar documents from Google and OpenAI — arguing that UX's value cannot be reduced to supplying context prompts for an AI system. The article lays out three concrete roles: embedding user-signal monitoring alongside technical metrics so that drops in task success trigger the same automated response as server errors; ensuring UX practitioners are pulled in whenever a spec raises a user-experience concern; and maintaining human judgment on the experience decisions that AI cannot reliably make. For research practitioners who are being squeezed out of early design stages in AI-first organizations, the piece offers a concrete vocabulary for arguing their way back in.

**[The Wrong Ambition for Synthetic Users](https://saeidehbakhshi.substack.com/p/synthetic-users-are-closer-to-wind) — Research toolbox (Oct 28, 2026)**
Saeideh Bakhshi argues that the field is so focused on getting AI to impersonate research participants that it is overlooking a more productive use: simulating the behavior of human-centered systems to understand how those systems will perform before they are built or changed. The distinction matters because impersonation and simulation require different validation standards and produce different kinds of evidence. For practitioners fielding pressure to replace participant research with synthetic data, the piece offers a useful reframe — one that concedes ground on simulation while holding the line on participant-based methods for insights about actual human behavior.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[Experience Design: Dropping the "User" from "User Experience"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-vs-experience-design/) — Nielsen Norman Group (Oct 30, 2026)**
Raluca Budiu proposes that the field adopt the term "experience design" to cover scenarios where AI agents act on a person's behalf — not just scenarios where a person uses a product directly. The framework splits scope into three situations: a human using a digital product, an agent acting for a human, and a human and agent working together through a shared journey. Handoffs between human and agent are flagged as a common point of experience breakdown. For researchers working on AI products, the practical implication is that evaluation needs to cover the full loop — not just the agent's task performance but whether the human's underlying goal was actually served and whether the handoff created confusion or friction.
