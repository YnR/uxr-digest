## 1. AI in the Research Practice

**[How to Decide When an AI Tool Is Worth Keeping — NN/G (Aug 7)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prove-framework/)**
Nielsen Norman Group introduced PROVE — a structured framework for evaluating whether to adopt an AI tool on a specific task. The acronym covers Problem Alignment, Risk, Output Quality, Velocity, and Experience, and is designed to produce a provisional, defensible decision rather than an organization-wide recommendation. The framing explicitly responds to the pressure teams face to adopt AI without clear evidence that a tool actually helps. This is immediately practical for researchers weighing the growing menu of AI-assisted planning, moderation, and analysis tools.

**[Can We Trust AI to Moderate UX Interviews? — MeasuringU (Aug 4)](https://measuringu.com/can-we-trust-ai-to-moderate-ux-interviews/)**
Jeff Sauro, Lucas Plabst, and Jim Lewis examine whether AI is ready to take over moderation — one of the most judgment-intensive tasks in qualitative research. The article notes that AI is already being deployed as moderator across usability testing, interviews, and contextual inquiry, and surfaces the fact that 419 professionals have signed letters against AI moderation of qual research. The piece is a methodologically grounded counterweight to vendor enthusiasm, and is worth reading alongside User Interviews' *Early Adopter's Guide to AI Moderation* for a fuller picture of where the debate stands.
