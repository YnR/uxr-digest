## 2. Research for AI-Powered Products

**[IA for AI, The UX of Contrast, Battling AI Fatigue — UX Collective (Fabricio Teixeira, Aug 2026)](https://uxdesign.cc/ia-for-ai-the-ux-of-contrast-battling-ai-fatigue-4f623495bed8)**
Fabricio Teixeira's weekly UX Collective roundup opens with a pointed framing: information architecture is the foundational discipline that AI-powered products most visibly lack, and the IA debt is now surfacing as a UX problem at scale. The newsletter also curates pieces on designing for contrast in AI interfaces and the emerging challenge of AI fatigue — users who are increasingly exhausted by the volume and opacity of AI features in products. For researchers on AI product teams, AI fatigue is worth treating as a first-class research question: measuring it, tracking it over product iterations, and distinguishing it from general feature fatigue.

## 3. Job Market for UX Researchers

**[Surviving the 2026 UX Research Job Market Shift — Philip Burgess](https://www.philipburgess.net/post/surviving-the-2026-ux-research-job-market-shift)**
*(Independent blog, not on the core monitored list — included because it surfaced prominently and is directly on-topic.)*
A well-evidenced post arguing that the perceived "decline" of UX research roles is real in quantity but not in quality: job postings for dedicated researcher roles have flattened or declined versus 2022–2023 peaks, but senior and strategically-positioned roles are holding. The market is bifurcating — commoditized execution skills are being absorbed by AI tools and embedded product/design roles, while strategic research capability (framing questions, influencing decisions, connecting findings to business outcomes) is the remaining differentiator. AI fluency is now a hard requirement at most hiring organisations, not a nice-to-have. The article's practical framing: 2026 is a maturing market, not a dying one, and the bar for entry has genuinely risen.
