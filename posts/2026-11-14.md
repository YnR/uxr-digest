## Focus Area 1: AI in the Research Practice

**[Research-In-The-Loop: How UXR Adapts to Development at AI Speed](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-in-the-loop/) — Nielsen Norman Group (Nov 13, 2026)**
Tony Alicea argues that the right response to AI-accelerated development isn't to squeeze research into smaller slots, but to redesign which questions get answered how. His proposed model divides research work into three categories: continuous evaluation (answered by instrumentation built alongside product features), independent discovery (open-ended questions requiring direct human involvement that instrumentation cannot replace), and pre-development research. A concrete practice he recommends is a lightweight "research section" for each feature — covering hypothesis, success criteria, harm criteria, what to track, and when to bring in participants — which embeds accountability into the development rhythm rather than treating research as a separate gate. The piece is notably direct that instrumentation supports but does not substitute for discovery: teams that eliminate the independent-discovery layer are not moving fast with research, they are removing it.

## Focus Area 2: Research for AI-Powered Products

**[Decision theatre or decision design?](https://uxdesign.cc/decision-theatre-or-decision-design-f1604f178685) — UX Collective (Nov 13, 2026)**
Patrizia Bertini coins "decision theatre" for the pattern where teams use AI-generated outputs to perform the act of deciding without the reflection that would make a decision trustworthy — AI supplies the information and the meeting ratifies it. The article grounds the concern in numbers: hallucination rates across 2026 models on a Vectara benchmark ranged from 1.8% to 24.2% of summaries, figures that vary by task and model but are high enough that treating any AI summary as reliable without verification is a systematic risk. For researchers designing evaluation frameworks or decision-support features, Bertini's three-part test is a useful checklist: does the team have the skills to judge AI output critically, is there a named authority who can override it, and does the workflow avoid the decision fatigue that causes people to defer to whatever the system suggests?
